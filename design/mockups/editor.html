Chapter 5: The Red Door

The rain hadn't stopped for three days. It drummed against the windowpane with a persistence that felt personal, a rhythmic accusation that Christie tried to ignore as she paced the small study.

"It's just water," she muttered, tracing the spine of an old encyclopedia. "Just atmospheric pressure and gravity."

But she knew it wasn't. The sky hung low and heavy, the color of a bruised plum. In the corner, the grandfather clock ticked—a slow, deliberate sound that seemed to stretch the silence between seconds. Tick... tock... tick...

She turned back to the desk. The letter sat there, unopened. The wax seal, stamped with a crest she didn't recognize, seemed to pulse in the dim light. A Griffin? A Dragon? It was too smeared to tell.

Joseph would know. Joseph always knew these things. But Joseph wasn't here. He was effectively a ghost now, a memory of a confident rich gentleman that haunted the hallways of her mind more than the house itself.

She sat down, the leather chair creaking in protest. Her fingers hovered over the keys of the typewriter, but the words wouldn't come. The tension in the room was palpable, a physical weight pressing against her chest.

Suddenly, a knock. Three sharp raps.

Christie froze. The front door was locked. The servants were gone for the weekend.

Another knock. Louder this time.
